Execution date: 2026-09-30. Prepared by focused_novelty for the coordinator.
Scope: continuation of E001; no broad source restart, no changes to references.bib/report.tex.

VERDICT

The normalized Hecke H_q(S3) length-damping obstruction is proved in this
project; an earlier occurrence of its exact projection certificate was not
located in this bounded audit. Do not label it novel, first, or a complete
solution to BCW Problem 9.1. The diagonal/proper qualification is essential
for the affine A2 continuation: affine Hecke C*-algebras are already type I,
whereas the inspected sources do not provide the requested proper semigroup
diagonal in the normalized canonical Hecke basis.

No prior exact classification of the real radial finite-A2 diagonal generator
cone was located. The general conditional-Choi machinery is standard; only
the specialized explicit inequalities/certificates could be project substance.

Current arXiv is v4, 18 September 2003:

Anchors in arXiv v4: Proposition 2.10 p.16 (finite over center),
Corollary 2.11(i),(iv) pp.16-17 (finite-dimensional irreducible
*-representations, dimension <= |W0|), Corollary 2.12 p.17 (C*-completion
finite type I). The older UvA 72-page PDF has a Lemma 2.7 at p.11, but
its numbering and stronger extension wording must not be mixed with v4.

Inference, not a claim about canonical multipliers: a separable finite
type-I von Neumann algebra decomposes into matrix-valued commutative
measure algebras. Increasing finite partitions supply finite-dimensional
conditional expectations converging in L2, hence Haagerup approximation.
One can form standard symmetric QMS using these expectations. None of
this forces the expectations or generator to be diagonal in {T_w}.
Therefore abstract QMS existence/Haagerup for affine A2 is not the
remaining research problem. The explicit proper diagonal rate constraint
remains the substantive question.

4. FINITE-DIMENSIONAL CONDITIONAL-CHOI CRITERION

Verified primary pedagogical source:
Michael M. Wolf, Quantum Channels & Operations: Guided Tour, lecture notes
dated 5 July 2012. Institutional record and archival PDF:
https://mediatum.ub.tum.de/1701036
https://mediatum.ub.tum.de/doc/1701036/1701036.pdf
Proposition 7.2, pp.122-123: for a Hermiticity-preserving map on M_d,
positivity of the Choi compression to the complement of the identity
vector is equivalent to a CP-plus-left/right-drift representation.
Proposition 7.3, pp.123-124: this is equivalent to generating a continuous
CP semigroup. These propositions do not require the generator's CP
part to be unital or its diagonal block to preserve trace. Unitality
and trace preservation are additional linear conditions.

The following direct-sum reduction is an elementary deduction from that
standard matrix criterion (not a separately located Hecke theorem).
Let A=direct_sum_i M_{d_i}, let L:A->A be Hermiticity preserving, and let
L_ij=pi_i L iota_j. Define
  C_ij(L)=sum_{a,b=1}^{d_j} E_ab tensor L_ij(E_ab),
and Omega_i=sum_a e_a tensor e_a (unnormalized),
  P_i=1-|Omega_i><Omega_i|/d_i.
Then exp(tL) is CP for every t>=0 iff
  C_ij(L)>=0 for i!=j,
  P_i C_ii(L) P_i>=0 for every i.
Necessity: at t=0 the cross-block Choi matrices vanish; their derivatives
must be PSD. The same derivative argument on the kernel of the diagonal
identity Choi matrix gives the compressed condition.
Sufficiency: the cross maps L_ij are CP. Proposition 7.2 writes each L_ii
as Phi_ii plus a left/right drift. Assemble the cross maps and Phi_ii into
a CP map Phi:A->A and the drifts into a single block-diagonal K in A.
Then L(x)=Phi(x)+Kx+xK*, with sign of K chosen according to convention.
The drift exponential is x->exp(tK)x exp(tK*), hence CP; exp(tPhi) is CP
by its power series; the finite-dimensional Lie-Trotter product proves
the assertion. This argument does not identify A with M_{sum d_i} and
does not replace the canonical Hecke trace by a uniform matrix trace.
For the positive rate operator D with semigroup exp(-tD), apply it to
L=-D. The coordinator should include this derivation or cite the matrix
propositions and explain the direct-sum deduction.
